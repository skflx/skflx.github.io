# Brief · `neurovascular` — Contents, rotation, arteries and planes (ch. 3, ~45 min)

**Title:** "The wiring". **tldr:**
- VIII turns about 90° inside the canal; only the fundus is fixed. [G3]
- The nervus intermedius leaves the brainstem between VII and VIII. [J9]
- VIII's transition zone lies 9–14 mm from the brainstem, VII's 2–3 mm; it is not where schwannomas start. [G4]
- The labyrinthine artery is an end artery, usually from an AICA loop. [G5, K6]
- The dissection plane between tumour and facial nerve is what saves the nerve; at the porus they cling. [HCA6]

## `nv-cpa` · From the brainstem to the porus
- **Teach:** VII and VIII leave the brainstem at the pontomedullary junction [K31; K6] and cross the CPA
  cistern to enter the porus as two structures: a tubular facial nerve and VIII, which enters as a single
  unit and divides inside the canal, identifiably only in the lateral 3–4 mm [K31]. In the canal the
  cochlear division runs anteroinferiorly beneath the facial nerve; the vestibular nerves lie in the
  posterior half [K31]. In retrosigmoid surgery the facial nerve is found more anteriorly along the
  brainstem; the courses vary [HCA5 p65].
- **Checks:** (1) "Where does VIII divide into separate nerves?" → in the lateral 3–4 mm of the canal.
  (2) "How do VII and VIII enter the porus?" → as two structures; VIII as one trunk.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The facial nerve traverses six distinct anatomical segments from the pons to the parotid gland`.
- **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `nv-cpa`.
- **Items:** (a) VIII divides in the lateral 3–4 mm. (b) cochlear division beneath VII.

## `nv-rotation` · The 90° turn
- **Teach** [G3, owner]: tracing fundus → brainstem the cochleovestibular bundle rotates about 90°, almost
  all of it inside the canal. Fundus: cochlear nerve in front of the inferior vestibular nerve; the
  cochleovestibular cleavage plane is vertical (superior–inferior). Mid-canal: the cochlear and inferior
  vestibular nerves fuse; the plane is poorly defined. Porus and CPA: the cochlear nerve lies inferior
  (anteroinferior) to the vestibular bundle; the plane is horizontal (anterior–posterior). Direction,
  fundus → brainstem, viewed from medial: right ear counterclockwise, left ear clockwise.
- **Figure:** F3 `layers` step (`rotation-*.svg`).
- **Call** `trap`, "180° is not normal anatomy": a 180° turn would put the cochlear nerve above the
  vestibular nerve at the brainstem [owner]. (Some atlases say 180°; `appraisal`.)
- **Deep · Chief**, "Variants": no cleavage plane in about a quarter (one fused trunk to the brainstem); in
  some the last 15–30° happens just medial to the porus [owner; Silverstein 1986; Schefter & Harner 1986].
  The facial nerve's own position varies too: in one cadaver series it stayed anterosuperior throughout in
  37% and turned 30–90° in 63% [Tian 2008].
- **Deep · Chief**, "Surgical consequence": selective vestibular neurectomy is most reliable laterally,
  before the nerves fuse; separating cochlear from inferior vestibular fibres mid-canal risks the cochlear
  nerve [owner].
- **Checks:** (1) "How far does the bundle turn, and where?" → about 90°, almost all inside the canal.
  (2) "At the brainstem, where is the cochlear nerve relative to the vestibular bundle?" → inferior.
  (3) "Right ear, fundus → brainstem, seen from medial: which way?" → counterclockwise.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none (`vestibular-schwannoma:q5` dropped: its explanation puts VII anterior at the fundus, against `fu-quadrants`). **Drill:** `nv-rotation`.
- **Items:** (a) ~90°, in the canal. (b) cleavage plane vertical at fundus → horizontal at CPA. (c) cochlear
  inferior at the brainstem. (d) right CCW / left CW (state the viewpoint in the stem). (e) no plane in ~25%.


## `nv-intermedius` · Nervus intermedius
- **Teach:** carries the facial nerve's parasympathetic and sensory fibres; it exits the pons between the
  motor facial trunk and VIII; how far it runs before joining VII varies [J9 figs 9.12–9.14]. Sensory
  fibres supply the posterior ear canal: hypesthesia there is the Hitselberger sign
  (`vestibular-schwannoma:q25`). Taste and secretomotor function: glossary note `nint`.
- **Checks:** (1) "Where does the intermedius exit the brainstem?" → between motor VII and VIII. (2) "What
  is the Hitselberger sign?" → hypesthesia of the posterior ear canal from its sensory fibres.
- **Ref:** `Sinus/Gustatory sensation is transmitted via the chorda tympani, greater superficial petrosal, glossopharyngeal, and superior laryngeal nerves` · `Otology/The first genu marks the location of the geniculate ganglion and origin of the greater superficial petrosal nerve`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q25`. **Drill:** `nv-intermedius`.
- **Items:** (a) exits between VII and VIII. (b) variable distance to joining VII.

## `nv-orz` · The transition zone
- **Teach** [G4, owner via Radiopaedia]: where central (glial) myelin changes to peripheral (Schwann)
  myelin over a few millimetres, the central myelin reaching further in the nerve's core. Distance from the
  root entry zone: VII 2–3 mm; VIII 9–14 mm. The zone is vulnerable to vascular compression (hemifacial
  spasm, vestibular paroxysmia; `cc-nvc`).
- **Call** `trap`, "The schwannoma myth": that vestibular schwannomas arise at the transition zone is an
  erroneous myth [Radiopaedia; Xenellis & Linthicum 2003]. They arise from vestibular Schwann cells within
  the canal (`cc-vs-origin`).
- **Call** `trap`, "Source conflict": one cadaver histomorphometry found VIII's central segment only 3.6 mm
  (VII 3.7 mm), its junction midway along the cisternal nerve [Gupta 2023].
- **Checks:** (1) "How far from the brainstem is VIII's transition zone?" → 9–14 mm (VII 2–3 mm).
  (2) "Do vestibular schwannomas arise there?" → no; that is a myth.
- **Ref:** `Otology/Vestibular schwannomas arise from the Schwann cell sheath of the inferior or superior vestibular nerve`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q8`. **Drill:** `nv-orz`.
- **Items:** (a) VIII 9–14 mm vs VII 2–3 mm. (b) myth of origin. (c) definition (glia → Schwann).

## `nv-aica` · AICA and its loops
- **Teach** [G5]: Chavda classification [McDermott 2003, PMID 12680822]: type I in the CPA; type II at the
  porus, up to half the canal; type III beyond half. In 664 sides: I 62%, II 30%, III 7.5%; types II and
  III were associated with unilateral hearing loss there. An AICA loop may lie in the canal [K2].
- **Figure:** F4 `layers` (`vessels-*.svg`).
- **Call** `trap`, "Source conflict": in 58 patients the Chavda type did not predict hearing loss; only a
  loop running between VII and VIII did [Gorrie 2010, PMID 19887989].
- **Deep · Senior**: distal AICA infarction after surgery gives ipsilateral dysmetria
  (`vestibular-schwannoma:q39`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Chavda type II?" → loop at the porus extending up to 50% of the canal. (2) "Commonest
  type?" → I (in the CPA).
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q39`. **Drill:** `nv-aica`.
- **Items:** (a) I/II/III definitions. (b) 62/30/7.5%. (c) conflict on hearing association.

## `nv-labyrinthine` · The end artery
- **Teach** [G5, G11]: the labyrinthine (internal auditory) artery usually arises from AICA's meatal loop
  (90% vs basilar 10% in 10 cranial bases [Haidara 2015, PMID 26242329]; the superior cerebellar artery is also possible
  [K6]); single trunk 60%, two 40% [Haidara 2015]. It divides into the anterior vestibular artery (utricle,
  superior and lateral ampullae) and the common cochlear artery (proper cochlear artery and the
  vestibulocochlear artery, whose vestibular ramus supplies the posterior ampulla, saccule and parts of the
  utricle) [K6, Mazzoni 1990]. In MCF surgery it typically runs between VII and the cochlear nerve, not
  visible, and must be preserved for hearing [HCA3 p45].
- **Call** `mech`, "End artery": no collateral, so its loss costs the inner ear (glossary `labyr-a`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Usual origin?" → AICA (its meatal loop). (2) "Its two divisions?" → anterior
  vestibular and common cochlear arteries.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `nv-labyrinthine`.
- **Items:** (a) origin. (b) the two divisions and territories. (c) between VII and cochlear nerve in MCF view.

## `nv-subarcuate` · The subarcuate artery
- **Teach:** emerges from the depression superolateral to the porus (subarcuate fossa) [K2]; lies in the
  centre of the superior canal's loop [J10 fig 10.9]. Coagulate and divide it before retracting the
  cerebellum: avulsion can tear AICA (glossary `subarc-a`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Where does the subarcuate artery enter bone?" → the subarcuate fossa, above and lateral
  to the porus, under the superior canal's arch. (2) "Why coagulate and divide it before retracting the
  cerebellum?" → avulsion can tear AICA.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `nv-subarcuate`.
- **Items:** (a) location. (b) why divide it.

## `nv-meninges` · Dura and arachnoid
- **Teach:** the posterior fossa dura lines the whole canal and merges with the nerves at their foramina
  [K2]; the subarachnoid space reaches the fundus (a fundal CSF cap on T2 MRI favours hearing preservation:
  HCA3 table 3.2; `im-fundal-cap`). With superior vestibular tumours the dissection plane lies between tumour
  and facial nerve, and the superior vestibular nerve is dissected away from VII to create it; at the porus
  VII is most adherent and thinnest [HCA6 p81].
- **Checks:** (1) "Where is the facial nerve most adherent to a schwannoma?" → at the porus.
  (2) "Where does the dura merge with the nerves?" → at their foramina.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `nv-meninges`.
- **Items:** (a) dura merges with nerves at foramina. (b) adherence at the porus.
