# Brief · `fundus` — The fundus, its crests and the meatal bottleneck (ch. 2, ~35 min)

**Title:** "The fundus". **tldr:**
- A horizontal crest everywhere; a vertical crest (Bill's bar) above it only. [K2, K31]
- Facial anterosuperior, superior vestibular posterosuperior, cochlear anteroinferior, inferior vestibular posteroinferior, singular canal below and behind. [K2, K31]
- The meatal foramen is the narrowest point of the facial canal: 0.68 mm. [K30]
- Learn the fundus once, then turn it for each view. [F2]

## `fu-crests` · Two crests
- **Teach:** The transverse (falciform, horizontal) crest divides the fundus into a smaller upper zone and a
  larger lower zone [K2]. The vertical crest, Bill's bar, divides only the upper zone: facial nerve in
  front, superior vestibular nerve behind [K2, K31; HCA3 p42]. Named for William House (glossary).
- **Call** `trap`, "Bill's bar is upper-zone only": it never divides the lower zone; the cochlear and
  inferior vestibular nerves are separated by position, not by a bar [K2].
- **Figure:** F2 frame 1 (`fundus-rs-right.svg`) as a single `hotspot` frame here (full explorer in `fu-views`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Which crest divides the fundus into upper and lower zones, and which divides only
  the upper?" → transverse (falciform) crest; vertical crest (Bill's bar). (2) "What does Bill's bar separate?" → facial nerve
  (anterior) from superior vestibular nerve (posterior).
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q3`. **Drill:** `fu-crests`.
- **Items:** (a) Bill's bar only in the upper zone. (b) upper zone smaller than the lower zone. (c) Bill's
  bar between VII (anterior) and SVN (posterior).

- **Also:** `figure` `images/oksat/iac-anatomy/stanford-fig-5.jpg` (the lateral IAC: SV, IV, 7, C, Bill's bar). Credit line in the caption: "Stanford Skull Base Surgery Atlas, © RK Jackler; used with the atlas's educational permission."

## `fu-quadrants` · Four nerves and their organs
- **Teach** [K2, K31, K6, G11]: anterosuperior: facial nerve → labyrinthine segment. Posterosuperior:
  superior vestibular nerve → utricle, superior and lateral canal ampullae, and the anterosuperior
  saccule (Voit's nerve) [K6; Büki & Ward 2021]. Anteroinferior: cochlear nerve, through a central canal
  ringed by small foramina into the modiolus [K2]. Posteroinferior: inferior vestibular nerve → the rest of
  the saccule [K6].
- **Call** `hook`, "Seven-up, Coke down": VII upper front, cochlear lower front. A mnemonic, not anatomy
  that holds medially (see `nv-rotation`).
- **Deep · Senior**, "The vestibular ganglion": Scarpa's ganglion sits at the lateral end of the canal,
  in superior and inferior parts matching the two nerves' territories [K6].
- **Checks:** (1) "Which nerve occupies the anterosuperior quadrant?" → facial. (2) "Which organs does the
  superior vestibular nerve supply?" → utricle, superior and lateral ampullae, anterosuperior saccule.
  (3) "Where is the cochlear nerve?" → anteroinferior.
- **Ref:** `Otology/cVEMP evaluates the saccule and inferior vestibular nerve via uncrossed sternocleidomastoid inhibition` · `Otology/oVEMP evaluates the utricle and superior vestibular nerve via crossed inferior oblique excitation` · `Otology/Vestibular neuritis causes acute spontaneous prolonged vertigo without auditory symptoms`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q4`. **Drill:** `fu-quadrants`.
- **Items:** one per quadrant (4) plus (e) Voit's nerve = SVN branch to the superior saccule.

## `fu-singular` · The fifth opening
- **Teach:** The singular canal lies posteroinferior to the inferior vestibular nerve and carries the
  posterior ampullary (singular) nerve to the posterior canal ampulla [K2, K31]. The singular nerve lies
  close to the round window membrane, the landmark for singular neurectomy [K2]. Singular neurectomy is
  seldom done today [J10 p402]. In translab the posterior canal ampulla marks the inferior border of the
  IAC [HCA6 p75].
- **Checks:** (1) "What runs in the singular canal?" → the posterior ampullary nerve. (2) "Which middle ear
  landmark leads to it?" → the round window membrane.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `fu-singular`.
- **Items:** (a) singular canal content. (b) round window as its landmark. (c) posterior canal ampulla
  marks the inferior IAC border in translab.

## `fu-cnc` · The bony cochlear nerve canal
- **Teach** [G6]: the short bony canal from the anteroinferior fundus into the modiolus [K2; glossary
  `bcnc`]. Normal diameter about 1.9–2.6 mm (casts 2.58 mm; CT 1.91–1.98 mm); < 1.4 mm suggests a
  cochlear nerve abnormality; > 3.0 mm suggests other anomalies [Stjernholm 2002, PMID 11876597].
- **Call** `trap`: "Cutoffs vary between series; < 1.4 mm is the one used here."
- **Checks:** (1) "Below what BCNC diameter should you suspect cochlear nerve deficiency?" → 1.4 mm.
  (2) "And above what diameter should you look for other anomalies?" → 3.0 mm.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `fu-cnc`.
- **Items:** (a) < 1.4 mm. (b) normal ~2 mm on CT.

## `fu-meatal` · The meatal bottleneck
- **Teach** [G2, K30]: the meatal segment runs from the porus to the meatal foramen, the narrowest point of
  the fallopian canal: **0.68 mm** on average [Fisch & Esslen 1972, via K30]. A periosteal band reinforces
  it and the nerve fills **83%** of the canal there, vs 73% tympanic and 64% mastoid [Proctor & Nager 1982,
  via K30]. The labyrinthine segment beyond it is the shortest and thinnest, **3–5 mm** [K30], bounded by
  the cochlea in front, the superior canal behind and the vestibule below [K2]. The arachnoid sleeve ends
  here (glossary `meatal-for`). The meatal foramen is the entrance of the labyrinthine segment, which is why
  sources call that segment "the narrowest" (as `facial-reanimation:q2` does).
- **Call** `mech`, "Why edema strangles here": a tight bony ring the nerve already nearly fills, so
  swelling becomes ischaemia; the greatest site of entrapment in Bell's palsy [K30] (→ `cc-bell`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Narrowest point of the facial canal, and its diameter?" → meatal foramen, 0.68 mm.
  (2) "What fraction of the canal does the nerve fill there?" → 83%.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The labyrinthine segment is the narrowest portion of the Fallopian canal and most vulnerable to edema`.
- **Practice:** `facial-reanimation:q2`. **Drill:** `fu-meatal`.
- **Items:** (a) 0.68 mm. (b) 83% vs 73/64%. (c) labyrinthine 3–5 mm with its boundaries.

## `fu-views` · Turning the fundus
- **Teach:** The same four nerves seen from medial (retrosigmoid), from lateral (translab), from above
  (middle fossa) and on oblique-sagittal MRI (anterior on the image left). Right and left mirror each other.
  From above, the facial nerve lies on top, in front; the cochlear and inferior vestibular nerves are
  below it [HCA3 p45; F2].
- **Figure:** F2 `hotspot`, all 8 frames, `modes: ['explore','find']`.
- **Checks:** (1) "In a right ear seen from medial, which side is anterior?" → the viewer's left. (2) "From
  the middle fossa, which nerve is on top?" → the facial nerve.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `fu-views`.
- **Items:** (a) right ear from medial: anterior on the left. (b) right ear from lateral: anterior on the
  right. (c) MCF view: VII superficial-anterior.
