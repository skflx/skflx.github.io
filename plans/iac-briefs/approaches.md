# Brief · `approaches` — Surgical corridors and perspective (ch. 5, ~50 min)

**Title:** "Entering the tube". **tldr:**
- Translab: from lateral, whole canal, hearing gone. [HCA6]
- Middle fossa: from above, facial nerve on top, hearing kept; tight at the fundus. [HCA3]
- Retrosigmoid: from behind, medial two-thirds only; the lateral third hides behind the posterior canal. [HCA5]
- Choice follows hearing, size and fundal extent; management lives in the schwannoma module. [K31, HCA3, HCA6]

## `ap-frame` · One fundus, three views
- **Teach:** the corridors see the same canal from lateral, above and behind (F6); the fundus explorer
  (`fu-views`) turns the nerves for each. Translab exposes the whole facial nerve from brainstem to
  stylomastoid foramen [HCA6 p68]; complete intratemporal exposure in a hearing ear needs a middle fossa
  plus transmastoid approach, in a deaf ear translab alone [J9 fig 9.23].
- **Figure:** F6 `layers` toggle.
- **Checks:** (1) "Which corridor sees the lateral third of the canal with hearing kept?" → middle fossa.
  (2) "How is complete intratemporal facial nerve exposure achieved in a hearing ear versus a deaf ear?" →
  middle fossa plus transmastoid in a hearing ear; translabyrinthine alone in a deaf ear.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `ap-frame`.
- **Items:** (a) which corridor sees what (match three).

## `ap-translab` · Translabyrinthine
- **Teach** [HCA6]: the most direct route to the CPA; hearing sacrificed; for any size when hearing is poor,
  and > 2 cm where hearing preservation is very unlikely (p68). Early facial nerve identification in the
  labyrinthine segment; tumour visible extradurally, little retraction. Inferior bone removal to the jugular
  bulb, around or in front of a high bulb (p75); drilling stops at the cochlear aqueduct, with IX–XI below
  (p77). Superior border marked by the superior canal ampulla, inferior border by the posterior canal ampulla
  (p75, p77). Apical cells above the canal are waxed (p78). The Eustachian tube is plugged and the middle ear
  obliterated against rhinorrhea (p77). At the fundus Bill's bar separates the facial canal from the superior
  vestibular canal; the facial nerve runs slightly superior as it leaves (p78); the superior vestibular nerve
  is stimulated, then divided and reflected posteriorly to open the facial nerve plane (p80–81).
- **Call** `pearl`, "Find the nerve before the tumour": a right-angle hook rises over the vertical crest to
  identify the labyrinthine facial nerve (p81).
- **Checks:** (1) "Inferior limit of translab drilling?" → the cochlear aqueduct. (2) "Which ampulla marks
  the superior border of the IAC?" → the superior canal ampulla. (3) "Why plug the Eustachian tube?" →
  against CSF rhinorrhea.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The translabyrinthine approach provides direct exposure of the internal auditory canal with complete hearing sacrifice`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q29`. **Drill:** `ap-translab`.
- **Items:** (a) cochlear aqueduct limit. (b) ampullae as borders. (c) ET plugging. (d) indication: poor
  hearing any size.

## `ap-mcf` · Middle fossa
- **Teach** [HCA3]: hearing preserving; House Clinic: tumours ≤ 2 cm with serviceable hearing; hearing
  preservation extremely unlikely > 2 cm (p40, p50–51). Elevate dura posterior to anterior because the
  geniculate is dehiscent in ~16% (p43). Start medially at the porus, the safe zone; medially 270° of bone can
  be removed (p42, p45). Laterally the cochlea lies ≤ 1 mm in front of the labyrinthine segment; the superior
  vestibular nerve can be followed half the labyrinthine length before the ampulla; ~2.5 mm from cochlear
  base to ampulla, so ≤ 2 mm burrs (p45, figs 3.6–3.7). The dura is opened posteriorly; the facial nerve lies
  in the anterior canal, on top; dissect medial to lateral to avoid avulsing cochlear fibres; preserve the
  internal auditory artery (p45). Middle meningeal artery = anterior limit (p41). Extended approach: watch
  the vein of Labbé (p49).
- **Call** `trap`, "Source conflict (size)": ≤ 2 cm (House Clinic) vs < 1.5 cm favouring retrosigmoid-
  retrolabyrinthine in the Sanna algorithm [K31]. Management: `vestibular-schwannoma`.
- **Call** `trap`, "The nerve is on top": from above, VII is the first structure over the tumour.
- **Checks:** (1) "Why elevate the dura from posterior to anterior?" → the geniculate is bare in ~16%.
  (2) "Where do you start drilling?" → medially, at the porus. (3) "What lies ≤ 1 mm in front of the
  labyrinthine segment?" → the cochlea.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The middle cranial fossa approach provides hearing preservation for intracanalicular schwannomas with lateral fundus extension`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q31`. **Drill:** `ap-mcf`.
- **Items:** (a) geniculate 16%. (b) start at the porus. (c) cochlea ≤ 1 mm. (d) ≤ 2 mm burr / 2.5 mm gap.
  (e) medial-to-lateral dissection protects cochlear fibres.

## `ap-mcf-landmarks` · Finding the canal from above
- **Teach** [G10]: *House*: follow the GSPN back to the geniculate, then the labyrinthine segment into the
  canal (House 1961; HCA3). *Fisch*: the canal's angle from the arcuate eminence / superior canal: 45–60°
  (HCA3 p44), about 60° (HCA3 p49), 73.5 ± 11.7° on CT [Sahana 2023]. *Garcia-Ibañez*: bisect the angle
  between the arcuate eminence and the GSPN: 126 ± 12° on CT, bisector 63 ± 6° [Sahana 2023]; judged the
  simplest and most reliable in a cadaver study [Maina 2007]. The arcuate eminence does not always sit over
  the superior canal [HCA3 p42].
- **Figure:** F5 `layers` toggle.
- **Call** `trap`, "Source conflict": all three angles have wide ranges [Sahana 2023; Maina 2007].
- **Checks:** (1) "Garcia-Ibañez method?" → bisect the arcuate eminence–GSPN angle. (2) "Why is the
  arcuate eminence unreliable alone?" → its relation to the superior canal is inconstant.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `ap-mcf-landmarks`.
- **Items:** one per method (3); (d) which is most reliable (Garcia-Ibañez, Maina 2007).

## `ap-retrosig` · Retrosigmoid
- **Teach** [HCA5]: hearing preserving, for tumours mainly in the CPA with little canal extension (p59).
  The porus is the focal point; drilling the posterior lip is limited by the posterior canal: 5 mm reliably,
  up to 10 mm in selected cases (p60); usually the medial two-thirds; the lateral third risks the vestibule or
  common crus (p63). The vestibular aqueduct is the lateral limit in hearing preservation (p67). Dissect
  medial to lateral; the facial nerve is found more anteriorly along the brainstem (p65). Watertight dural
  closure is generally impossible; cells at the posterior lip are waxed and covered with fat; leaks are
  commoner because apical cells are hard to seal; headache may come from bone dust (p65–66). Residual tumour
  at the fundus ~7% vs 0% translab [K31].
- **Call** `trap`, "The blind spot": the lateral third of the canal is behind the posterior canal and common
  crus.
- **Checks:** (1) "How much canal can be exposed safely?" → 5 mm reliably, up to 10 mm. (2) "Lateral limit
  in hearing preservation?" → the vestibular aqueduct. (3) "Why more CSF leaks?" → apical air cells are hard
  to seal.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The retrosigmoid suboccipital approach allows hearing preservation for small to medium vestibular schwannomas`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q30`, `vestibular-schwannoma:q38`. **Drill:** `ap-retrosig`.
- **Items:** (a) 5/10 mm. (b) medial two-thirds. (c) vestibular aqueduct limit. (d) fundal residual 7% vs 0%.

## `ap-extend` (`deep · Chief`) · Beyond the three
- **Teach:** the transcochlear approach extends translab by rerouting the facial nerve and drilling out the
  cochlea (wiki note); an auditory brainstem implant bypasses the cochlea and cochlear nerve (wiki note);
  selective vestibular nerve section and singular neurectomy are seldom done today [J10 p402]; vestibular
  neurectomy is most reliable laterally, before fusion [owner, G3].
- **Checks:** (1) "Where is selective vestibular neurectomy most reliable, and why?" → laterally, before the
  nerves fuse. (2) "How does the transcochlear approach extend translabyrinthine exposure?" → by rerouting
  the facial nerve posteriorly and drilling out the cochlea.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The transcochlear approach expands the translabyrinthine corridor through posterior facial nerve rerouting and cochlear drill-out to access the prepontine space and ventral clivus` · `Otology/Auditory Brainstem Implantation ABI bypasses the cochlea and cochlear nerve to stimulate the cochlear nucleus in NF2`.
- **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `ap-extend`. **Items:** (a) neurectomy laterally (one item).

## `ap-choose` · Choosing by anatomy
- **Teach:** poor hearing → translab, any size [HCA6]; serviceable hearing, small, intracanalicular →
  middle fossa (House: ≤ 2 cm) [HCA3]; serviceable hearing, CPA-dominant, no fundal extension →
  retrosigmoid [HCA5; K31]; fundal extension and a CSF cap matter (`im-fundal-cap`). Meningiomas displace
  rather than invade VII and VIII, so hearing preservation may be chosen even for large ones [HCA6 p69].
  Everything else (observation, radiosurgery) belongs to `vestibular-schwannoma`.
- **Checks:** (1) "Serviceable hearing, tumour filling the fundus: which corridor reaches the fundus?" →
  middle fossa (retrosigmoid cannot see the lateral third). (2) "Why may a hearing-preserving corridor still
  be chosen for a large CPA meningioma?" → meningiomas displace rather than invade VII and VIII.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The translabyrinthine approach provides direct exposure of the internal auditory canal with complete hearing sacrifice` · `Otology/The middle cranial fossa approach provides hearing preservation for intracanalicular schwannomas with lateral fundus extension` · `Otology/The retrosigmoid suboccipital approach allows hearing preservation for small to medium vestibular schwannomas`.
- **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `ap-choose`.
- **Items:** (a) three short vignettes, one per corridor. (b) meningioma displacement → hearing approach possible.
