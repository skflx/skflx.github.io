# Brief · `osteology` — Bony architecture and the six walls (ch. 1, ~35 min)

**Title:** "The bony box". **tldr:**
- About 1 cm long, 4–8 mm wide, in line with the ear canal. [G1]
- Porus medially (sharp posterior lip), fundus laterally against the otic capsule. [K2, K31]
- Six walls, six hazards: middle fossa above, bulb and cochlear aqueduct below, cochlea and carotid in front, posterior canal and vestibular aqueduct behind. [HCA3, HCA6, K2]
- Air cells around the canal are the route of CSF leaks. [HCA5, HCA6]

## `os-orient` · Which way the canal points
- **Teach:** The petrous pyramid points anteromedially toward the clivus (K2: "the pyramid pointing
  anteromedially"). The IAC runs mediolaterally, its long axis in line with the external auditory canal
  [G1: K2, K31, HCA3 p42]. In the middle fossa approach this is how the surgeon orients: the IAC lies on
  roughly the same axis as the ear canal [HCA3 p42].
- **Call** `trap`, "The pyramid is not the canal": the canal does not follow the pyramid's long axis.
- **Figure:** F8 `layers` (`iac-axis-base.svg`, layers `axis`, `canal`).
- **Checks:** (1) "From the middle fossa, what external landmark gives the canal's axis?" → the external
  auditory canal: the IAC lies on roughly the same axis. (2) "Does the IAC run along the long axis of the
  petrous pyramid?" → No; the pyramid points anteromedially, the canal runs mediolaterally.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `os-orient`.
- **Items:** (a) IAC axis is collinear with the EAC → MCQ. (b) pyramid points anteromedially, canal
  mediolaterally → MCQ.

## `os-dev` · Where the nerves come from
- **Teach** [G14, K1]: In week 4 the acousticofacial ganglion splits: its dorsal part becomes the
  geniculate ganglion of VII, its ventral part the statoacoustic ganglion. The statoacoustic ganglion's
  superior part forms the superior vestibular nerve; its inferior part forms both the inferior vestibular
  nerve and the cochlear ganglion. The sensory patches split the same way: superior → utricle and the
  superior and lateral canal cristae; inferior → saccule and posterior canal crista. The otic capsule
  ossifies from week 15, from 14 centres, fused by week 23 without sutures. The cochlear aqueduct forms
  between weeks 9 and 40 from a remnant of the inferior cochlear vein; it is not a true duct.
- **Call** `mech`, "Why the inferior nerves stay together": the inferior vestibular nerve and the
  cochlear ganglion share an origin, which fits their tight fusion mid-canal (`nv-rotation`). [K1, G3]
- **Checks:** (1) "Which nerve does the superior part of the statoacoustic ganglion form?" → the superior
  vestibular nerve. (2) "Which end organs does the inferior part supply?" → saccule and posterior canal
  crista (and it forms the cochlear ganglion).
- **Ref:** `Otology/Inner ear malformations are classified by Jackler and Sennaroglu based on embryonic arrest timing and cochleovestibular architecture`.
- **Practice:** none (do not use `pediatrics` q5: its "week 16" conflicts with K1). **Drill:** `os-dev`.
- **Items:** (a) superior statoacoustic ganglion → SVN; inferior → IVN + cochlear ganglion. (b) otic capsule:
  week 15 start, 14 centres, fused by week 23. (c) cochlear aqueduct is a vein remnant, not a true duct (recall).

## `os-zones` · Porus, canal, fundus
- **Teach:** The porus is the opening on the posterior face of the petrous bone; its posterior border is an
  acute angle, its anterior border flatter [K2, K31]. Posterior fossa dura lines the canal its whole length
  and ends by merging with the nerves at their foramina [K2]. The fundus is the lateral end, where the
  crests and foramina are (detail in `fundus`) [K2]. In middle fossa surgery the porus is the "safe zone"
  to start drilling, the fundus the tight end [HCA3 p42].
- **Checks:** (1) "Which lip of the porus is sharp?" → posterior (acute angle). (2) "Where does the
  canal's dural lining end?" → by merging with the nerves as they enter their foramina at the fundus.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q3`. **Drill:** `os-zones`.
- **Items:** (a) posterior porus border acute, anterior flatter. (b) dura merges with nerves at the foramina.

## `os-morph` · How big, and when to worry
- **Teach** [G1]: About 1 cm long [K2, K31]; CT in 162 patients: length 11.0–11.1 mm, mid-canal diameter
  4.2 mm, no sex or age difference [Gözen 2026, PMID 41682992]. Normal diameter 4–8 mm, measured
  perpendicular to the long axis at mid-canal or the porus [owner; Marques 2012]. Stenosis: < 2 mm is the
  classic cutoff, linked to aplasia or severe hypoplasia of VIII; < 3 mm in broader screening ("near-narrowed
  IAC") [owner; El Sadik 2017; Ionescu 2023]. Flaring > 8 mm (some series > 9 mm) [owner]. Side-to-side
  difference > 2 mm is abnormal and calls for contrast MRI [owner].
- **Call** `pearl`, "Walls tell you why": symmetric, smooth-walled flaring with a sharp transverse crest
  favours a variant or dural ectasia; asymmetric widening with erosion favours a mass [owner].
- **Call** `trap`, "A normal canal can hold an absent nerve": about a third of deficient cochlear nerves sit in
  a normal-calibre canal [owner; McClay 2008]. (Detail in `im-cnd`.)
- **Deep · Senior**: "Where the numbers come from": Marques 2012 measured 110 normal CTs: adult vertical
  diameter 4.47 mm, opening width 7.10 mm, length 9.84 mm; children slightly larger in length and
  diameter [PMID 23329967]. Cutoffs vary between series (`appraisal`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Normal IAC diameter range?" → 4–8 mm. (2) "Classic stenosis cutoff, and its link?" →
  < 2 mm; VIII aplasia or severe hypoplasia. (3) "What side-to-side difference is abnormal?" → > 2 mm.
- **Ref:** none. **Practice:** none. **Drill:** `os-morph`.
- **Items:** (a) normal 4–8 mm. (b) stenosis < 2 mm (classic), < 3 mm (screening). (c) asymmetry > 2 mm →
  contrast MRI. (d) symmetric smooth flaring with sharp crest → variant/ectasia (vs asymmetric erosion → mass).

## `os-walls` · Six walls, six hazards
- **Teach** (each wall one short paragraph):
  - *Superior:* middle fossa floor and dura; the arcuate eminence roughly over the superior canal (an
    inconstant relation); GSPN and geniculate anteriorly; superior petrosal sinus medially along the
    ridge [HCA3 p41–42].
  - *Inferior:* the jugular bulb, whose height varies and which can reach the canal [K2]; the cochlear
    aqueduct, the inferior and anterior limit of translab drilling, with IX–XI just below it [HCA6 p77; K2].
  - *Anterior:* the basal turn of the cochlea, ≤ 1 mm in front of the labyrinthine facial nerve [HCA3 p45];
    the petrous carotid anterolaterally [HCA3 p49]; the petrous apex medially.
  - *Posterior:* posterior fossa dura; the vestibular aqueduct and endolymphatic sac under the operculum,
    inferolateral to the porus [K2]; the posterior canal and common crus laterally, which limit
    retrosigmoid drilling [HCA5 p60, p63]. The endolymphatic duct opening marks the posterior canal [K2].
  - *Lateral:* the vestibule and cochlea of the otic capsule [K2].
  - *Medial:* the porus opening onto the cerebellopontine angle [K2].
- **Figure:** F1 `hotspot` (`iac-walls-box.svg`, explore), then a `neighbors` block on `iac`.
- **Call** `trap`, "The bulb sets the floor": a high jugular bulb can lie almost against the canal and
  limits the inferior trough [K2; HCA6 p75].
- **Checks:** (1) "What is the inferior limit of translab drilling, and what lies below it?" → the cochlear
  aqueduct; IX, X, XI. (2) "What limits retrosigmoid drilling of the posterior lip?" → the posterior canal
  (and common crus laterally). (3) "What lies ≤ 1 mm in front of the labyrinthine segment?" → the cochlea.
- **Ref:** `Otology/The otic capsule is an immutable endochondral bone that provides fixed surgical reference landmarks`.
- **Practice:** none (`vestibular-schwannoma:q2` asks about the CPA, not the walls). **Drill:** `os-walls`.
- **Items:** one per wall (6): superior → arcuate eminence/GSPN/SPS; inferior → bulb, cochlear aqueduct
  above IX–XI; anterior → cochlea ≤ 1 mm, carotid; posterior → vestibular aqueduct/sac, PSCC/common crus;
  lateral → vestibule/cochlea; medial → CPA.

## `os-cells` · Air cells around the canal
- **Teach:** Cell tracts of the temporal bone: posterosuperior (sinodural), posteromedial (retrofacial and
  retrolabyrinthine), subarcuate, perilabyrinthine (supra- and infralabyrinthine), peritubal [K2]. Highly
  pneumatized bones carry more risk of postoperative CSF rhinorrhea [K31]. In translab, apical cells above
  the IAC must be waxed [HCA6 p78]; in retrosigmoid, cells opened at the posterior lip are waxed and
  covered with fat because apical cells are hard to seal [HCA5 p65–66].
- **Call** `mech`, "Why a closed dura still leaks": CSF follows opened cells to the middle ear and out the
  Eustachian tube (full story in `cc-csf`).
- **Checks:** (1) "Name the perilabyrinthine tracts." → supra- and infralabyrinthine. (2) "Which bones leak
  more after IAC surgery?" → highly pneumatized ones.
- **Ref:** `Otology/Cerebrospinal fluid CSF leak and pseudomeningocele require lumbar drainage, fat obliteration, and re-exploration`.
- **Practice:** `vestibular-schwannoma:q35`. **Drill:** `os-cells`.
- **Items:** (a) the five tract groups (recall). (b) pneumatization raises leak risk.
