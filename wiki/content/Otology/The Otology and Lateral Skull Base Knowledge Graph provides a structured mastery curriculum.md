## Tier I: Bedrock Foundations — Cochlear Micromechanics, Auditory Electrophysiology & Vestibular Testing
1. The traveling wave along the basilar membrane establishes tonotopic frequency organization
2. Outer hair cell electromotility driven by prestin amplifies cochlear micromechanics
3. The stria vascularis generates the endocochlear potential of positive eighty millivolts
4. The six auricular hillocks of His derive from the first and second branchial arches to form the pinna
5. The retrotympanum is partitioned into sinus tympani, facial sinus, and subtympanic sinus by the ponticulus and subiculum
6. Tuning fork tests utilize the 512-Hertz fork to differentiate conductive from sensorineural hearing loss via Weber and Rinne tests
7. Tympanometry classifies middle ear compliance and pressure into Jerger Types A, As, Ad, B, and C
8. Acoustic reflex thresholds and reflex decay testing identify retrocochlear lesions and facial nerve pathology
9. Inner ear malformations are classified by Jackler and Sennaroglu based on embryonic arrest timing and cochleovestibular architecture
10. Ewald's laws and Alexander's law govern the directional dynamics and intensity of vestibular nystagmus
11. Auditory brainstem response waves I through V identify retrocochlear and brainstem pathology
12. Wave I of the ABR arises from the distal auditory nerve while wave V originates at the lateral lemniscus and inferior colliculus
13. Electrocochleography SP to AP ratio exceeding zero point four indicates endolymphatic hydrops
14. Cervical and ocular vestibular evoked myogenic potentials test saccular and utricular otolith function
15. cVEMP evaluates the saccule and inferior vestibular nerve via uncrossed sternocleidomastoid inhibition
16. oVEMP evaluates the utricle and superior vestibular nerve via crossed inferior oblique excitation
17. The video head impulse test vHIT identifies high-frequency angular vestibulo-ocular reflex deficits
18. Overt and covert saccades on vHIT indicate peripheral vestibular hypofunction
19. Bithermal caloric testing calculates Jongkees formula to quantify unilateral vestibular paresis
20. Rotary chair testing assesses low-frequency bilateral vestibular hypofunction and central compensation
21. Audiometric masking with the plateau method prevents shadow responses from the non-test ear
22. Otoacoustic emissions evaluate outer hair cell integrity and differentiate cochlear from auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder
23. Acute otitis externa is managed with topical antimicrobial-steroid drops and aural debridement
24. Auricular hematoma evacuation and bolster compression prevents permanent cauliflower ear deformity
25. Acute perichondritis of the auricle requires antipseudomonal therapy to prevent cartilage necrosis
26. Traumatic tympanic membrane perforations heal spontaneously in the majority of cases without early surgery
27. External auditory canal foreign bodies require atraumatic extraction under microscopic guidance to protect the tympanic membrane
28. The otic capsule is an immutable endochondral bone that provides fixed surgical reference landmarks
29. The cochleariform process serves as the constant middle ear landmark triangulating the geniculate ganglion, tympanic CN VII, and tensor tympani tendon
68. The infant mastoid lacks a developed tip placing the facial nerve in a subcutaneous vulnerable position at the stylomastoid foramen
69. The greater and lesser petrosal nerves function as parasympathetic couriers connecting middle ear nerves to the lacrimal and parotid glands
70. Keratosis obturans and external auditory canal cholesteatoma differ in clinical presentation, bone erosion patterns, and systemic associations

## Tier III: Cholesteatoma Mastercraft, Canal Wall Up vs Down & Epitympanic Surgery
31. Primary acquired cholesteatoma arises from retraction pocket invagination of the pars flaccida into Prussak space
32. Secondary acquired cholesteatoma develops from squamous epithelial migration through a tympanic membrane perforation
33. Congenital cholesteatoma presents as an anterosuperior white mass behind an intact tympanic membrane
34. Canal wall up mastoidectomy preserves the posterior bony external auditory canal wall
35. Canal wall down mastoidectomy lowers the facial ridge and exteriorizes the mastoid into the ear canal
36. Lowering the facial ridge flush with the facial nerve is essential to prevent dependent cavity pooling
37. Wide meatoplasty provides adequate aeration and easy cleaning of a canal wall down mastoid cavity
38. Non-echo-planar diffusion-weighted MRI detects recurrent and residual cholesteatoma as small as two millimeters
39. Second-look surgery or non-EPI DWI MRI is indicated twelve to eighteen months after canal wall up mastoidectomy
40. Labyrinthine fistula from cholesteatoma erosions most frequently involves the lateral semicircular canal
41. Management of a labyrinthine fistula requires preservation of the matrix until complete bony exposure
42. Tegmen erosion with dural exposure increases the risk of meningoencephalocele and CSF leak
43. Petrous apex cholesteatoma requires infracochlear, translabyrinthine, or middle cranial fossa exteriorization
44. Auto-mastoidectomy occurs when chronic expansive cholesteatoma exteriorizes spontaneously into the canal
45. Transcanal endoscopic ear surgery TEES provides line-of-sight visualization into the sinus tympani and epitympanum

49. Stapedotomy utilizes a micro-drill or laser fenestration of the posterior footplate
50. Laser stapedotomy reduces mechanical trauma and footplate mobilization compared to manual perforators

54. Bone-anchored hearing implants BAHA stimulate the cochlea directly via bone conduction pathways

56. Cochlear implant candidacy criteria include severe to profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss
57. Round window and extended round window insertions minimize intracochlear acoustic trauma
58. Scala tympani electrode array placement preserves residual hearing and avoids basilar membrane translocation

## Tier V: Vestibular Disorders, Meniere Disease, BPPV & SCDS
61. Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo BPPV results from canalithiasis or cupulolithiasis in semicircular canals
62. The Dix-Hallpike maneuver produces torsional upbeating nystagmus in posterior canal BPPV
63. The Epley particle repositioning maneuver clears otoconia from the posterior canal into the utricle
64. Horizontal canal BPPV is diagnosed with the supine roll test and treated with the Lempert barbecue maneuver
65. Meniere disease is characterized by episodic vertigo, fluctuating low-frequency SNHL, tinnitus, and aural fullness
66. Endolymphatic hydrops results from impaired resorption of endolymph within the endolymphatic sac
67. Low-sodium diet, hydrochlorothiazide, and betahistine form the first-line medical therapy for Meniere disease
68. Intratympanic dexamethasone and gentamicin provide non-ablative and chemical ablative control of Meniere vertigo
69. Endolymphatic sac decompression and shunt surgery alleviate hydrops while preserving auditory function
70. Superior semicircular canal dehiscence SCDS presents with Tullio phenomenon and sound-induced vertigo
71. Third mobile window pathophysiology causes hyperacusis to bone-conducted sounds and acoustic energy dissipation
72. High-resolution temporal bone CT with Poschl and Stenvers reformatting confirms superior canal dehiscence
73. Middle cranial fossa and transmastoid canal plugging or resurfacing repair superior canal dehiscence
74. Vestibular neuritis causes acute spontaneous prolonged vertigo without auditory symptoms
75. Labyrinthitis presents with acute prolonged vertigo combined with sudden sensorineural hearing loss
76. Pulsatile tinnitus is differentiated into arterial and venous etiologies by auscultation, jugular compression, and targeted vascular imaging

## Tier VI: Facial Nerve Disorders, Decompression, Neuroma & Reanimation
76. The facial nerve traverses six distinct anatomical segments from the pons to the parotid gland
77. The labyrinthine segment is the narrowest portion of the Fallopian canal and most vulnerable to edema
78. The first genu marks the location of the geniculate ganglion and origin of the greater superficial petrosal nerve
79. House-Brackmann grading system stratifies facial nerve function from Grade I normal to Grade VI total paralysis
80. Electroneuronography showing greater than ninety percent degeneration within fourteen days indicates severe facial nerve axonal injury
81. Electromyography EMG detects voluntary motor unit potentials and fibrillations indicating denervation
82. Bell palsy causes acute idiopathic facial paralysis and responds to early high-dose oral corticosteroids
83. Ramsay Hunt syndrome herpes zoster oticus presents with severe facial paralysis, otalgia, and auricular vesicles
84. Temporal bone longitudinal and transverse fractures cause delayed or immediate facial paralysis
85. Transmastoid and middle cranial fossa facial nerve decompression relieves canalicular compression
86. Facial nerve schwannomas present with slow progressive paralysis or facial twitching along the Fallopian canal
87. Immediate direct epineurial end-to-end repair is the gold standard for transected facial nerve injury
88. Hypoglossal-to-facial nerve anastomosis restores resting facial tone and voluntary smile with tongue movement
89. Masseteric nerve transfer to facial nerve branches restores rapid, powerful dynamic smile reanimation
170. Upper eyelid gold or platinum weight implantation prevents exposure keratopathy in lagophthalmos
171. Acute Bell's palsy presents with unilateral lower motor neuron facial paralysis treated with early oral steroids
172. The Seddon and Sunderland classifications grade peripheral nerve injury from Class I neuropraxia to Class V neurotmesis
173. Topodiagnostic testing localizes facial nerve injury through Schirmer testing, stapedius reflexometry, and electrogustometry

94. The retrosigmoid suboccipital approach allows hearing preservation for small to medium vestibular schwannomas

98. Fisch classification categorizes temporal bone and skull base paragangliomas from Class A to Class D
99. Fisch infratemporal fossa approach Type A mobilizes the facial nerve and exposes the jugular foramen
100. Preoperative embolization reduces intraoperative blood loss in hypervascular glomus jugulare tumors

## Tier S: Otologic Emergencies, Clinical Audiology & Vestibular Medicine
1. Otogenic intracranial complications escalate from meningitis to sigmoid sinus thrombosis and brain abscess
2. Gradenigo syndrome combines petrous apicitis with abducens palsy and deep retro-orbital pain
3. Otogenic sigmoid sinus thrombosis presents with picket-fence fevers and the Griesinger sign
4. Perilymphatic fistula causes fluctuating hearing loss and pressure-induced vertigo after barotrauma or stapes surgery
5. Spontaneous CSF otorrhea from tegmen dehiscence requires multilayer middle fossa or transmastoid repair
6. Congenital cholesteatoma presents as an anterosuperior white mass behind an intact tympanic membrane
7. Congenital aural atresia candidacy is graded by the ten-point Jahrsdoerfer scale
8. Eustachian tube dysfunction divides into dilatory and patulous forms with opposite treatment goals
9. Adult unilateral otitis media with effusion mandates nasopharyngoscopy to exclude nasopharyngeal carcinoma
10. Tympanostomy tube placement follows guideline criteria for chronic effusion and recurrent acute otitis media
11. Presbycusis produces symmetric high-frequency sensorineural loss with disproportionate speech-in-noise decline
12. Noise-induced hearing loss produces a four-kilohertz notch and is governed by damage-risk exchange rates
13. Platinum and aminoglycoside ototoxicity mandates baseline and serial high-frequency audiometric monitoring
14. Hearing aid selection is driven by audiometric configuration and verified with real-ear measurement
15. Chronic subjective tinnitus is managed with cognitive behavioral and sound-based therapy rather than cure
16. Pulsatile tinnitus mandates structured evaluation for vascular and third-window etiologies
17. Autoimmune inner ear disease causes rapidly progressive bilateral sensorineural loss responsive to corticosteroids
18. The HINTS examination separates vestibular neuritis from posterior circulation stroke in acute vestibular syndrome
19. Vestibular migraine is the most common cause of episodic spontaneous vertigo
20. Persistent postural-perceptual dizziness is a chronic functional vestibular disorder treated with rehabilitation and SSRIs
21. Bilateral vestibulopathy causes oscillopsia and dark-dependent imbalance from bilateral hair cell loss
22. Vestibular rehabilitation exploits adaptation habituation and substitution to recalibrate gaze and posture
23. Internal auditory canal lipomas demonstrate T1 hyperintensity with fat saturation signal loss and mandate conservative observation
