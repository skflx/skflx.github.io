About

the short version

I started losing my hearing when I was 13. By the end of medical school, I received a cochlear implant. Today, as a resident training in otolaryngology and head and neck surgery, I have a rare vantage point: I know what the auditory system sounds like from both sides of the electrode.

That dual perspective drives much of what I do academically. My primary research focuses on bimodal cochlear implant users. Specifically, I'm investigating binaural pitch fusion to predict who will actually benefit from bilateral implantation before they even reach the operating room. Beyond otology and auditory neuroscience, I'm passionate about modernizing surgical education and exploring how emerging technologies can reshape our field.

My path here wasn't exactly a straight line. I grew up moving between Kathmandu, Thailand, Michigan, and Oregon, trained in Nevada and New Haven, and eventually found my way back to the Pacific Northwest. When I'm not at the hospital, you can usually find me with ink on paper, a book in hand, picking heavy things off the floor, or frolicking through the forests and waterfalls of the PNW.

Research

hearing science & how surgeons learn

My research sits at two poles: auditory neuroscience, with personal stakes, and surgical education, with theoretical ones. My published work spans H&N oncology and craniofacial trauma; my active work is in CI outcomes and how trainees learn to operate.

Otology & hearing sciences
Binaural pitch fusion as a pre-surgical predictor of bilateral CI candidacy; CI outcomes in bimodal users.
Surgical education
Live-streaming and automated feedback systems for training.
Head & neck oncology
Complications, risk factors, and survival outcomes in cancer surgery.
Craniofacial trauma
Injury patterns in sports and factors associated with surgical outcomes.
